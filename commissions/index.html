Commissions

A drawing of your animal, made once.

Crystal Orlando accepts commissions for original charcoal and graphite drawings of horses, dogs, wildlife and other subjects. She works from your high-resolution photographs or from a reference shoot she makes herself. A 50% non-refundable deposit reserves your place on the schedule; the balance is due on completion.

How it works

  1. Tell her the subject

    Email a few words about the animal or scene, the size you have in mind, and where the piece will hang. Attach your best photographs or ask about a reference shoot.

  2. Agree the piece

    Crystal proposes a surface (paper, canvas or cradleboard), a size, a price and a realistic timeline. Nothing is booked until you are happy with all four.

  3. Reserve your place

    A 50% non-refundable deposit holds your slot on the schedule and covers materials. Large canvas pieces are booked in order of deposit.

  4. The drawing

    Progress photos are shared at milestones. Because charcoal cannot be corrected, the work is slow and deliberate; the timeline you were given already accounts for that.

  5. Approval and delivery

    You approve the finished drawing from photographs. The balance is paid, the piece is fixed and packed, and it ships insured or is collected from the studio or a gallery.

What makes a good commission

The strongest portraits come from photographs that show the animal as you know it: ears forward, weight shifting, the look it gives you at the gate. Several sharp images in natural light, taken at the animal's eye level, give Crystal the structure she needs. She sees "not just a subject, but all the shapes that form the subject," and a good reference lets those shapes come through.

Where photographs fall short, she can travel to make her own reference for a fee. That is how the bison drawings began: a herd in Wyoming she was able to approach within a few yards, photographed once, and drawn many times since.

Surfaces and sizes

Paper suits intimate portraits and is framed under glass. Canvas and cradleboard suit large pieces and hang unglazed, the way a painting does. Both are fixed by the artist before delivery.

Commission questions

Before you write

What can I commission?

Any animal or subject you care about: a horse, a dog, a bull, wildlife you photographed, a family member on horseback. Crystal specialises in animals but has drawn figurative and classical subjects.

What photographs do you need?

Sharp, high-resolution photographs taken in natural light, ideally several angles and one that shows the expression you want captured. Phone photos work if they are large and unedited. If your photos are not strong enough, Crystal can travel to shoot her own reference for a fee.

How does payment work?

A 50% non-refundable deposit reserves your place on the schedule and covers materials. The remaining 50% is due when the finished drawing is approved and before shipping.

How long does a commission take?

It depends on the size and the current queue. Small works on paper are quickest; large charcoal-on-canvas pieces take considerably longer because the medium cannot be corrected and each area is built up slowly. You receive a realistic timeline before paying the deposit.

Can I choose canvas or paper?

Yes. Paper suits smaller, intimate portraits and is framed under glass. Canvas or cradleboard suits larger pieces and hangs without glass, the way a painting does.
